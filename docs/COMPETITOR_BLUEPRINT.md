## 1. Purpose and evidence rules

This document defines how HOIQUANWEB will identify and evaluate direct and indirect competitors without inventing market facts. It is not a completed competitor study.

No competitor names, market shares, rankings, traffic estimates, prices, customer counts, strengths, weaknesses or performance claims are asserted in this draft. A competitor set must be selected from the actual service scope, target geography and audience. Those inputs are not yet sufficiently specified.

### 1.1 Confirmed context

- HOIQUANWEB is a multi-page technology/service website.
- It serves both B2C and B2B, with B2C first and B2B expansion later.
- Vietnamese and English are required; Vietnam and international markets are in scope, with English-speaking markets prioritized.
- Seven service groups are retained: Website, Software, Mobile App, Game, SEO, AI & Automation and Other Technology Services.
- Primary conversion is consultation and quotation request.
- No public USP or comparative claim is approved for Core Preview (DEC-013). Use factual service descriptions only.

### 1.2 Evidence hierarchy

1. Dated primary evidence from a competitor's official public website, product documentation, public pricing or published case study.
2. Reputable independent research with transparent methodology and date.
3. Search result / SERP observations recorded with query, language, country, device and date.
4. Third-party SEO estimates, clearly labelled estimates and never treated as first-party facts.
5. Hypotheses, clearly marked as unverified and excluded from public copy.

For each observation record URL, capture date, geography, language, device/context, evidence excerpt or screenshot reference, evaluator and confidence. Re-check time-sensitive details before publication.

## 2. Competitor classification

### 2.1 Direct competitors

Organizations that demonstrably offer one or more of the same service categories to a materially overlapping audience and geography. Classification requires evidence of service overlap, target market and operating model.

### 2.2 Indirect competitors

Alternative ways a customer can solve the same need, such as in-house teams, freelancers, software platforms, no-code tools, agencies specializing in a subset of services, or managed providers. Include only where the substitution relationship is evidenced.

### 2.3 Substitute / status-quo alternatives

Doing nothing, using existing internal processes, delaying a project, or using an existing tool may be relevant alternatives. These are customer choices, not named competitors.

### 2.4 Competitor inclusion criteria

A candidate is included only when:
- It has a verifiable public identity and active official presence.
- At least one of the seven service groups overlaps with HOIQUANWEB's confirmed offer.
- Audience and geography overlap is plausible and supported.
- The evidence is recent enough for the evaluated attribute.
- The candidate is not included solely because it appears in an arbitrary search list.

The number of competitors and final shortlist remain TBD.

## 3. Research scope and comparison dimensions

| Dimension | What to inspect | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Business / market position | Stated audience, service focus, geography and business model | Official pages and dated supporting evidence |
| Service coverage | Which of the seven service groups are actually offered | Specific service pages, not navigation labels alone |
| UI comparison | Visual hierarchy, typography, color use, responsive patterns and clarity | Dated desktop/mobile captures |
| UX comparison | Navigation, service discovery, forms, accessibility and friction | Repeatable task walkthrough and device context |
| SEO comparison | Indexable architecture, page quality, metadata, technical signals and SERP presence | Crawl/sample URLs, SERP record and dated tools |
| Feature comparison | Publicly described capabilities and customer-facing workflows | Official feature descriptions or product docs |
| Content comparison | Content usefulness, depth, language quality and editorial quality | Representative page/article samples |
| Trust comparison | Verifiable credentials, project evidence, reviews and policies | Source URL and evidence of authenticity |
| Conversion | CTA clarity, contact options, inquiry steps and disclosed friction | Public journey walkthrough; do not submit real personal data |
| Pricing / commercial terms | Publicly disclosed prices, quote model and scope | Official current pricing page or written public terms |

Do not infer service quality from visual polish, SEO tool scores or competitor marketing language.

## 4. Research protocol

For every candidate:
1. Record the reason for inclusion and the service overlap.
2. Capture official website and relevant service URLs.
3. Record date, country/market, language and device.
4. Review one representative journey per relevant service.
5. Record visible UI and UX evidence, not subjective conclusions alone.
6. Review representative indexable pages and search appearance.
7. Record public price information only if explicitly published.
8. Separate observed facts, third-party estimates and evaluator interpretation.
9. Identify limitations, missing evidence and confidence.
10. Have the Project Owner review any proposed positioning or public comparison.

A single screenshot or third-party score is not sufficient evidence for a broad claim.

## 6. SWOT framework

No evidence-backed SWOT conclusion is approved yet. Use this framework after research:

- **Strengths:** internal capabilities demonstrated by evidence and confirmed operational scope.
- **Weaknesses:** internal limitations, missing proof, constraints or capability gaps confirmed by the Owner.
- **Opportunities:** unmet customer needs, underserved segments or channel gaps supported by research.
- **Threats:** external competition, substitutes, platform changes, regulation or market risks supported by evidence.

Do not label generic assumptions (for example, “lower price”, “better quality” or “faster delivery”) as strengths or opportunities.

## 7. Competitive strategy guardrails

- Core Preview must use factual service descriptions and must not publish unsupported USP/comparative claims.
- Competitor observations are internal planning evidence until the Project Owner approves any public use.
- Do not copy competitor text, designs, proprietary assets, brand identifiers or protected content.
- Differentiate through verified scope clarity, useful content, accessible UX and reliable inquiry handling only when those qualities are demonstrated and accepted.
- Do not promise price leadership, superior performance, guaranteed rankings, delivery speed or outcomes without evidence and explicit approval.
- Do not create competitor landing pages or comparison pages in P0 without a separately approved requirement, legal review and original evidence.

## 9. Acceptance criteria

- Direct, indirect and substitute categories are defined.
- Every named candidate has a documented inclusion rationale and verifiable sources.
- UI, UX, SEO, feature, content and trust comparisons use repeatable criteria.
- SWOT statements trace to evidence or confirmed internal facts.
- Findings distinguish observations, estimates and interpretation.
- No competitor claim is copied into public messaging without approval and verification.
- Research date, market, language, device and confidence are recorded.
- Project Owner approves the shortlist and any positioning derived from the research.
